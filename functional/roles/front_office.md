# Front office: roster, recruitment, trading and contracts

NFL teams generally **draft, trade and sign** players under roster and salary-cap rules, rather than literally "buy" them in a transfer market.

## Job to be done
Give a decision-maker a **reliable scouting input** about fit and hidden value, not fabricate a dollar valuation from tracking data.

## Highest-value questions / KPIs

| Question | Signal | Required data | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which player fits a required style? | Receiving/run behavior, role-specific comparable profile | Tracking + player roles + plays | Shortlist for scouts |
| What contribution is missing from box score? | Off-ball association or opportunity proxy, with caveats | Multi-player tracking, targets | Commission extra film review |
| Is performance stable or situational? | Performance across meaningful contexts with uncertainty | Repeated plays/context | Gauge data confidence |
| What are cost and availability? | Contract years, salary-cap impact, roster constraints | **External contract/cap data (probably NOT present)** | Only if independently licensed, verified and explicitly allowed |

## Minimum functional behavior
- Two-player comparison with fair context and sample counts (if data allows).
- Clear **football evidence** distinct from monetary or contractual analysis.
- Limitations banner: limited season/game sample, role mismatch, unobserved QB/OL context.

## Decision supported
"Who should our recruitment department investigate further?" **Not:** "Sign this player for $X."

## STOP / skip
Do not compute salary recommendations, trade values, injury predictions or risk scores without independently validated, legally usable data and a defensible model.

## Done when
An executive can explain why a player enters the scouting shortlist and what evidence remains missing.
