# Coaches and tactical / opponent analysts

## Job to be done
When preparing a game plan or reviewing film, identify **repeatable situations worth examining**, not just a striking single play.

## Highest-value questions / KPIs

| Question | KPI / evidence | Needed data | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| When does a receiving option actually get space? | Separation vs time, annotated at QB throw moment | Receiver/defender frames, event times | Identify tactical windows |
| Do movements affect another receiver's space? | Off-ball movement and defender shift co-occurrence, with caveat | Multiple offensive/defensive tracks | Guide film review of route concepts |
| Which spatial weak spots repeat? | Zone-low-proximity frequency across comparable plays + counts | Defender tracks, context, multiple plays | Prioritize opponent film study |
| Does an observed pattern change by situation? | Breakdown by down/distance/play family **if present** | Context, adequate subgroup sizes | Avoid misleading overall averages |

## Minimum functional behavior
- Select a comparable-play group and show the exact inclusion criteria.
- Trace from an aggregate signal to 1–3 auditable plays.
- Annotate timing, field direction and sample size.
- State clearly that **nearest defender ≠ assigned coverage** and **open zone ≠ exploitable play call**.

## Decision supported
"What film and situation should our staff review next?" **Not:** "This play will beat that defense."

## STOP / skip
If multiple defender trajectories or meaningful comparable plays are missing, do not market defensive weakness detection. Downgrade to descriptive single-play replay only.

## Done when
A coach can point to one repeatable, data-supported behavior, understand the reference moment, and articulate a follow-up film-review question. No invented strategy recommendation.
